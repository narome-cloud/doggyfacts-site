Why dogs pant: your dog sweats through its feet

Published September 15, 2026 · Reviewed September 16, 2026

Dogs can't sweat like people do. Their tongue does most of the cooling work.

Humans cool down by sweating all over the body. Dogs can't. The American Kennel Club explains that the sweat glands that help dogs cool off (merocrine glands) are found in their paw pads. That is nowhere near enough surface to release heat on a warm day.

So dogs pant. When a dog pants, air moves quickly over the wet surfaces of the tongue, mouth and airways, and the evaporating moisture carries heat away.

The tongue also works like a radiator. In a classic study, Krönert and Pleschka (1976) measured blood flow to the tongue of dogs at rest and while panting in the heat. Blood flow rose about five and a half times, and the heat the tongue released rose about eight times. Blood arrives warm, cools down at the tongue, and returns to the body.

What you can do

  • Always give your dog shade and fresh water on warm days.
  • Heavy panting at rest, or panting with drooling, weakness or confusion, can be a sign of heatstroke or pain. Call your vet.

Sources

  • American Kennel Club – Do Dogs Sweat?
  • Krönert H. & Pleschka K. (1976). Pflügers Archiv 367: 25–31 (PubMed 1034283)
