How we make our videos

Short videos, real sources, clear AI disclosure, and a human who approves every video.

1. Choosing a topic

We look at what dog owners are searching for: questions like "why do dogs pant" or "how to stop my dog jumping on people". We use public search suggestions and the YouTube Data API to see which questions are growing, and we don't repeat a topic.

2. Research and sources

Every script is built on a source we can link: a peer-reviewed study (for example in Applied Animal Behaviour Science, Animal Behaviour or JAVMA) or guidance from organizations like the American Kennel Club and VCA Animal Hospitals. If we can't find a solid source, we change the topic. Sources are listed in each video description and on the dog facts pages.

3. Producing the video

Scripts are written in English. Visuals and narration are generated with AI tools, and some effects (like the "thermal view" in our panting video) are artistic illustrations, not real measurements. Every video is marked as AI-generated or altered content on YouTube, TikTok and Instagram, and labeled "AI illustration" on screen.

4. Review and publishing

The channel owner watches each finished video and approves it before it is published. Nothing goes live without that approval.

The Doggy Facts publishing tool

Doggy Facts uses a small internal tool to upload our own approved videos to our own accounts. It is not a public service and it is not used for any other creator.

  • TikTok: the owner signs in with TikTok (Login Kit) and authorizes the app. The tool reads the basic profile (display name and avatar) to confirm the right account, uploads the approved video with the Content Posting API, and publishes it with the caption the owner approved, marked as AI-generated content.
  • YouTube and Instagram: the tool uses the official APIs with the owner's authorization, in the same way.
  • Tokens are stored privately, used only to post our own content, and can be revoked at any time from the platform's settings.

The owner's sign-in page for the tool is at app.html. It is only used by the Doggy Facts team.
